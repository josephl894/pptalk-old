# Competitive landscape

Last updated 2026-05-04.

## Direct (slide → narrated video)
| Tool | Geo | Strength | Weakness | Threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **aiorator (PPTalk China)** | CN | Validated demand, polished UX, voice cloning | Chinese-only, may port to US | High if they port |

## Adjacent — avatar-driven video
| Tool | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Synthesia** | Avatar quality, enterprise sales | Avatar-only, slow, expensive ($30+/mo) |
| **HeyGen** | Voice + avatar, fast | Avatar-first, deck workflow is afterthought |
| **D-ID** | Avatar API | Developer-tier, no end-user product |

## Adjacent — AI video, general
| Tool | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Pictory** | Long-form → short-form video | Not slide-driven; output is generic stock footage |
| **InVideo** | Templates, scale | Not narration-focused |
| **Descript** | Editor UX, transcript-driven | Doesn't ingest decks; podcaster-leaning |

## Adjacent — slide generation (different problem!)
| Tool | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Tome** | Beautiful AI slides | Generates, doesn't narrate existing |
| **Gamma** | Slide + doc + web from prompt | Same |
| **Beautiful.ai** | Smart templates | No narration |

## Wedge hypothesis
US AI video tools split into two camps: **(a) generate-the-deck-for-you** (Tome, Gamma) or **(b) talking-head avatar** (Synthesia, HeyGen). Almost no one focuses on *"I have a deck, narrate it well, output a clean video"* — which is exactly what aiorator nailed in China.
